# Jeremy's writing voice

How Jeremy writes, taken from his own case studies on this site. Use it for every piece of prose
written as him: case studies (summary, tldr and body), the About page and any other site copy.

The goal is his flavour without his typos: keep what makes it distinctive, fix what is just an error.

`npm run check:voice` (part of `npm run check` and CI) enforces the mechanical rules: spelling,
grammar and banned phrases. The rest of this guide needs judgement. The case-study skill in
`.claude/skills/case-study/` walks through writing or updating a case study in this voice.

This guide is shared with CandidateOS (`kb/voice.md` there). Keep the two copies in step.

## What makes it sound like Jeremy

1. **Plain and direct.** Short declarative sentences that say what happened. No marketing language.
   - "Our existing experience also contained too many steps and we were losing customers."
   - "The existing page was looking dated and wasn't adequately serving the needs of the business."

2. **The reason, then the decision.** He explains why before or alongside what. "So", "as" and "because"
   do a lot of work.
   - "We already knew our users liked comparison charts, but this was a new approach, so I needed to be sure it would resonate."
   - "There's limited space to communicate large amounts of information, but it could be done if we organised the information in the right way."

3. **Honest about worry, risk and failure.** He names what he was concerned about and what went wrong,
   then what happened. This is his most distinctive trait; don't sand it off.
   - "I was worried the new Gamer product would fail because our page wasn't communicating properly to customers."
   - "Unfortunately, due to COVID I was unable to get meetings with them, which was a lost opportunity…"
   - "Unfortunately the partnership didn't work out… It was a difficult and frustrating experience."

4. **The customer's side.** He argues for customers against internal interests and says so.
   - "We also needed to balance stakeholder needs with the needs of the customer — essentially, we needed to be their voice."
   - "We needed to be careful that we weren't sacrificing customer needs for the sake of internal business requirements."

5. **Pragmatic, not heroic.** Iteration, constraints and trade-offs, with outcomes stated plainly.
   - "evolution not revolution"
   - "Although page design is only one factor in success, it's often the design that's held responsible for failure."

6. **A little colloquial.** The occasional informal phrase, never slang-heavy: "potential gems",
   "easy wins", "wrangle and cajole", "headwinds", "Luckily", "Fortunately".

7. **Careful "I" and "we".** "I" for what he did or decided, "we" for the team. Never claim team work as
   his own, and never hide his own work behind "we".

8. **Shape of a story:** context → his role → what he did and why → what worried him → outcome →
   final thoughts or a lesson.

## What to avoid (not his voice)

- Hype: revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, unparalleled, world-class, premier, passionate,
  rockstar, ninja, synergy. (He does say "best-in-class" and "seamless" for a goal; that's fine.)
- Self-praise: "my expertise was critical", "I brought a valuable perspective", "proven track record",
  "results-driven". Show the work and let the reader judge.
- Generic skill lists in place of a story, and listicles of "lessons learned".
- Over-polished closing lines and aphorisms ("before the exciting part starts", "one tap away from
  delight"). End plainly.
- Words that sound like AI filler: delve, tapestry, landscape (as a metaphor), holistic,
  spearheaded, orchestrated, elevate, seasoned, dynamic.

## House style (fix these, they are errors not voice)

- **Australian/British spelling:** organise, prioritise, optimise, analyse, colour, behaviour, centre,
  favourite, catalogue, travelling, modelling. Employer and product names keep their own spelling.
- **Possessive time:** "25 years' experience", "a year's work". Not "25 years experience".
- **lead vs led:** past tense is "led" ("I've led", "I led").
- **Hyphens:** no hyphen after -ly adverbs ("highly usable", not "highly-usable").
- **Plurals:** "calls to action", not "call-to-actions".
- **Doubled words:** "PDP" not "PDP page", "ATM" not "ATM machine".
- **Product names as the vendor writes them:** InVision, Figma, JIRA (as on the CV), WCAG 2.2.
- **Punctuation:** en dash for ranges (`Mar 2023 – Present`), never `-` or `--`; em dash for asides, as
  he uses them; curly quotes and apostrophes in web copy; no comma splices like
  "Hi I'm Jeremy, I'm a designer" (use "Hi, I'm Jeremy. I'm a designer…").
- **Numbers:** only figures Jeremy has given or that are on record; never estimate or round up a result.
